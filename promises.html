Welcome to the page of Promises and Implementation

What were the historic terms of Treaty 1?

Treaties were signed with certain entitlements and promises. These entitlements are mainly focused on First Nations people who were part of the Treaty and reside on the land in which the Treaty was signed in.

The promises that were made in the signing of Treaty 1 include each family of five recieving 160 acres of land (About for 32 acres of land per family member), a school being maintained on each reserve of Treaty 1, pay each family of 5 a total of 15 dollars per year (3 dollars per family member), and lay aside and reserve land to be exclusively used by First Nations people on the lots of land they agreed to reserve for them.

How is Treaty 1 being implemented today?

Treaty 1 is not only something from the past. Today, it is still being implemented through Indigenous businesses, urban reserves, housing projects, and economic development in Manitoba.

Naawi-Oodena Urban Reserve

Naawi-Oodena means "Centre of the Heart and Community" in Anishinaabemowin. It is owned by the seven Treaty One First Nations and officially became reserve land in 2022.

The project shows how Treaty 1 continues today by supporting:

  • Indigenous-owned businesses
  • New jobs and economic growth
  • Housing and community spaces
  • Partnerships with the City of Winnipeg
  • Future opportunities for First Nations communities

The development plan includes commercial buildings, housing, recreation spaces, and community services.

Block A Development

The first phase of the project is called Block A.

One of the main developments is the Oodena Gas Bar, an Indigenous-owned gas station and convenience store located near Kenaston Boulevard and Taylor Avenue in Winnipeg.

This project helps create jobs, business opportunities, and economic growth for Treaty One Nations.

Urban Reserves Today

Another way Treaty 1 is implemented today is through urban reserves.

An urban reserve is reserve land located inside or near a city. These areas allow First Nations communities to build businesses and participate in the local economy while keeping Indigenous ownership and governance.

Examples include:

  • Naawi-Oodena Urban Reserve
  • Long Plain First Nation businesses
  • Indigenous-owned gas stations and commercial projects

These developments help support employment, investment, education, and community growth.
